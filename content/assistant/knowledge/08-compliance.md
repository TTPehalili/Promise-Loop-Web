# Calling by the rules

General information, not legal advice. When asked a legal question, say what PromiseLoop's calls are built around, and add that the visitor's own counsel should confirm for their situation.

PromiseLoop enforces the rules before a rep can dial, even on the client's own phone system.

- Opening: reps open as part of the client's team ("This is Ana, calling for Lone Star HVAC with PromiseLoop."), say the call is recorded, and confirm they're speaking with the right person before mentioning any bill. On consumer accounts they give the required notice: "This is an attempt to collect a debt, and any information obtained will be used for that purpose." Then they explain what the bill is for and where the client's company is based.
- Hours: only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the customer's local time, and never at a time the customer says is inconvenient. PromiseLoop blocks calls outside those hours, even on the client's dialer.
- Frequency: no more than 7 call attempts in 7 days per account, and no repeat call within 7 days after a conversation. Stricter state limits apply automatically, such as 2 calls in 7 days for Massachusetts customers.
- No robocalls or autodialers: the dialer is set to manual for PromiseLoop reps, and a person places every call. No prerecorded or artificial-voice messages on real customer calls.
- Work calls: not if the customer asks PromiseLoop not to, or if PromiseLoop knows the employer doesn't allow it. It's noted on the account and the system enforces it.
- Disputes: the account is paused right away and the dispute goes to the client. The client provides the supporting documents, the customer gets verification, and calls resume only after it's resolved.
- Stop means stop. Customers can also limit how they're reached, such as "evenings only" or "email only", and PromiseLoop follows it.
- No threats: PromiseLoop doesn't sue, report to credit bureaus, or threaten either. It doesn't invent deadlines or add fees the client's agreement with the customer doesn't allow.
- Texts and emails go out through the client's own tools, from the client's numbers and addresses, logged in one place. Every email includes an easy way to opt out. Texts go only to customers who agreed to receive texts; replying STOP ends them right away.
- Voicemails: only the rep's first name, a business name and a callback number. No details about any bill, in case someone else hears it.
- Recording: calls are recorded for quality and accuracy, and every customer hears that at the start of the call. Some states require everyone on a call to agree to recording.
- Business (commercial) customers: the same respect and the same hours. Some consumer-only rules, like the debt collection notice, don't apply to commercial accounts, but the standards don't change.
- Written notice: after the first conversation, customers receive a written notice with the amount owed, who it's owed to, and how to dispute it, as Regulation F requires.
- Fine print: PromiseLoop provides outsourced accounts receivable calling services designed to support compliance with the FDCPA, Regulation F, the TCPA and applicable state laws. The client remains responsible for the accuracy and validity of the accounts it sends. This is not legal advice.

## do_not_say

- "Fully compliant", "guaranteed compliant", "guarantees compliance", "100% compliant"
- Legal advice, or "yes, this is legal in your state"
- State rules not listed here (don't invent other states' limits)
- Certifications, badges or licenses not listed here
- That calls are not recorded, or that recording needs no notice
